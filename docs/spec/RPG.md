# Agent Town — Action RPG and Dungeon Expansion

Story, characters, missions and rewards for an action-RPG layer under Tallyford: a 60-floor dungeon crawler beneath the Old Exchange, weapons and Ledgercraft magic, status effects, gear and backpack upgrades, companions, and a deeper fishing game — all built on the farming economy and the one-way bridge from the earlier tabs.

## 1. How the expansion fits

The town stays cozy; the danger lives below it. Everything above ground — the farm, the square, the firm, the festivals — keeps the rules of the design spec, and the action-RPG layer opens through one door in the Old Exchange's cellar and three wild zones at the map's edges. The firm is still the engine, the bridge still runs one way, and nothing a sword or a spell does can touch an agent's limits.

**What this tab changes in the earlier tabs:**

1. The Vault Below grows from 30 floors in three biomes to **60 floors in five wings** with a boss every 12 floors, the elevator every 6 (after the Prospectors' Hall), and the endless Deep Stacks beneath floor 60 (§2). This supersedes the Vault paragraph in the mini-games section and §12.2 of the prompt.
2. Inventory becomes a **backpack progression** (12 → 24 → 36 → 48 slots) plus a tool belt, instead of a flat 36 (§8).
3. Two skills join the five: **Arms** (weapons) and **Ledgercraft** (magic), with their own professions (§4, §5). Stewardship is unchanged.
4. Mini-game 2 is replaced by the full combat system in §4; fishing (mini-game 1) gains rods, tackle, bait, the Vault pool and five legendary hunts (§9).
5. The pillar "cozy first, no death" is kept with one precise wording: **a knocked-out character is carried home, never killed**; the only loss is a tithe (§4.4).
6. Phases 10–12 are appended to the delivery plan (§12); Phases 0–9 are unchanged.

**Safety line for this tab.** Weapons, spells, loot, companions and missions are in-game only: no gear, buff or mission reward reads or writes an agent's limits, tier, allocation, permission or model; live agents never fight, never join the party and never appear in the Vault; companion AI is scripted, never a model; the "honest ledger" counters in §10 come only from in-game choices, never from permission decisions.

## 2. The story below the town

The Ledger Bank did not simply collapse twenty years ago; it was **sealed**. When the board saw the books could not survive an audit, they locked the counting halls beneath the Old Exchange, blamed the clerk Marguerite Tally, and left. Debt does not sit still in the dark. Every unpaid entry the board buried became a **Debt** — a thing of paper, ink and coin that walks the halls counting what it is owed — and the deeper the halls, the older and larger the debts. The Vault Below is the bank's buried body, and its five wings are the five offices of the five board members, each still kept by the shape of the person who ran it.

The Founder goes down for three reasons that become one: ore and gems for the town above, Marguerite's scattered Old Ledger pages that prove the cover-up, and — once the Tallies start chiming on the stairs — to settle the bank's books by hand, floor by floor, until the last page is recovered from the hands of **The Receiver** on floor 60.

**Three wild zones** feed the same systems above ground, with the same enemies at lower density and no bosses: **Hollow Wood** (east of the Far Bank; night and Fall; Paper Moths, Clerk Husks, the Hollow Night maze), **the Far Bank Marsh** (Marsh Leeches that inflict Spoiled; Ledger Carp fishing; Old Ledger pages in the reeds), and **the Highlands Crags** (Crag Hawks, Coin Golems that fell from the tram line; the Lantern Fruit tree). Town, farm and Firm Hill are safe zones; no enemy ever appears within sight of the firm.

**How the plot deepens.** Act I ends when the Teller falls and Nana O admits the Tallies were the bank's own bead-strings, hidden by Marguerite the night of the sealing. Act II is the Archives and the Reading Room: each keeper, beaten, drops a page that names a board member, and Sterling Vance recognises the Broker's contracts as the template Meridian still uses. Act III runs through the Treasury to the Undercount, where the Receiver holds the last page — and the town's name on a debt the board signed in Tallyford's name. The Charter path (six halls) and the Receiver's defeat combine into a **third ending, *The Books Balanced***: the Exchange reopens, the debt is struck from the ledger on the steps at dawn, and the Tallies carry the last page to the Founder's Audit. The Meridian path can still beat the Receiver (*Settled Out of Court*: Meridian buys the debt and the halls stay grey), and the Charter path without the Receiver stays *Charter Restored*.

**The party.** The Founder leads a party of up to three: themself plus two companions. Companions are **residents the player created** (any weapon class, chosen at the Armory) or the two townsfolk who will go below — **Juniper** (Tally Hammer, aggressive, breaks shields) and **Constable Ada Ferrers** (Abacus Flail, defensive, pulls enemies off you, only available 22:00–06:00). Companions have HP, a weapon, one spell, and a trait; they gain Arms XP, Trust hearts (a run together = a talk and +40), and lines on the stairs; at 0 HP they sit out the floor and rejoin at the next landing. Live agents are never companions and never enter the Vault; they will, however, comment from the firm's windows when the player comes up with a boss's page ("Sterling just went very quiet about those contracts").

## 4. Combat

Real-time, top-down, eight-direction action with one attack button, one dodge, one spell slot and a charged move per weapon class — readable telegraphs over reflexes, so a farmer can clear Copper with a Copper Blade and a tactician can clear the Undercount with Quill Daggers.

### 4.1 Controls and feel

1. Attack (Space / A): a three-hit combo for Blades and Daggers, a single heavy swing for Hammers, a sweeping arc for Flails, a shot for the Sling. Holding the button charges the class move (§7). Inputs buffer for 150 ms so combos never drop.
2. Dodge (Shift / B): a 0.35 s roll with 0.25 s of invulnerability and a 0.8 s cooldown; no Vigor cost. Hammers block instead (hold): damage −70%, and a block in the first 0.2 s is a parry that Stamps the attacker.
3. Spell (Q / X): the equipped Entry (§5); a second slot opens at Ledgercraft 5.
4. Hit-stop of 60 ms on every landed hit, a 2-px camera shake on heavy hits, a brief white flash on the enemy; reduce-motion mode removes shake and flash and keeps the sound.
5. Enemies telegraph every attack for 0.4–0.8 s with a pose and a sound; nothing in the Vault hits without a tell.

### 4.2 Stats and formulas

| Stat | Base | Grows by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heart (HP) | 100 | +10 per Arms level, +20 Lantern Fruit (first three only), armor, Quartz Seal |
| Ink (mana) | 60 | +5 per Ledgercraft level, Aquamarine Seal; regen 1/s out of combat, 0.3/s in combat |
| Defense | 0 | Armor, Bulwark profession, Insured status |
| Vigor | 270 | Unchanged; each floor costs 6, each wild-zone entry 10 |

Damage dealt = weapon roll × (1 + 0.05 × Arms level) × class modifier × crit (×2, chance 5% + weapon + Moonstone). Damage taken = enemy damage − defense, minimum 1; Depreciated and Audited modify the result. Arms XP: 5 per kill (×2 for keepers' adds, 200 per keeper); Ledgercraft XP: 2 per cast that hits or heals.

### 4.3 Difficulty modes (per save, switchable in town)

1. **Peaceful** — no enemies; hazards only (collapsing shelves, blinding dust, gusts, coin traps); keepers appear as puzzle rooms (hit the drawer, break the umbrellas, answer the Comptroller's sums); all loot and pages still drop.
2. **Standard** — the roster below.
3. **Hard Audit** — enemies +40% HP, statuses last ×1.5, keepers gain one extra phase, Deep Stacks scale faster; cosmetic title and a gold ledger frame; no extra Marks.

### 4.4 The knockout rule

At 0 Heart the character is knocked out, not killed: Tallow (or Ada at night) carries them to the Chapel, where they wake at 06:00. The **tithe** is 10% of the Marks carried on the run (cap 500 ₥) and 10% of the run's ore and loot; weapons, armor, spells, pages and companions are never lost. Companions knocked out sit the floor out. No save is ever deleted and no progress rolls back; the only thing a bad run costs is the run.

## 5. Ledgercraft: the magic system

Magic in Tallyford is bookkeeping done with intent: a spell is an **Entry** written in Ink, and the four schools are the four things a ledger can do — pay out, take away, bring to balance, and reveal. Nana O teaches the first Entry after the Teller falls; the rest are found as scrolls on the floors, sold by Tallow for Old Ledger pages, taught by Brother Osric, or dropped by keepers. A learned Entry levels with use (I → III at 25 and 100 casts: −20% Ink, +25% effect).

## 9. Fishing expansion

Fishing becomes its own progression with four rods, nine tackle and bait types, two new waters, a fish pond, traps, and five legendary hunts that end in a catch sequence closer to a boss fight than a bite. The bar-catch mini-game from the design spec is unchanged; everything here changes what you cast with, where, and for what.

**Rods** (Ansel): Reed Rod (start; 1 tackle slot) · Ash Rod (1,200 ₥; bar +12 px) · Spring-Steel Rod (3,500 ₥; bar +24 px, 2 tackle slots, can hold bait and tackle) · Moonsilver Rod (forged: 5 Moonsilver ore + Spring-Steel Rod; bar +32 px, fish escape 30% slower, casts into the Vault pool without a lantern).

**Tackle** (one or two slots): Spinner (bite time −40%) · Sinker (fish stays in the lower half) · Trap Bobber (bar drains 50% slower when the fish escapes) · Treasure Hook (treasure +20%) · Lantern Lure (night fish bite by day) · Ink Bobber (required in the Vault pool; cures Inked while fishing) · Cork Float (floaters stay low). **Bait:** Bug Bait (crafted from moths; bite −25%) · Wild Bait (crafted: bug bait + fiber + Leech Gum; chance of a double catch) · Inkworm (Vault floors 25+; the only bait Mister Ink takes) · Tally Bait (festival prize; any fish bites).

**Waters.** The Ford and docks, the Far Bank (ferry), the Highlands lake (tram) as before; new: the **Marsh pools** (Far Bank; Ledger Carp, Marsh Pike, leeches on the line) and the **Vault pool** on floors 30 and 54, where Ghost Pike, Blind Char and the Ink Leviathan live and every cast risks a Paper Moth swarm.

**Fish pond** (5,000 ₥ + 200 stone, Riverside Plot): holds one species, breeds to 10 over 14 days when fed, produces roe every 3 days (Pressed Roe at the Preserves Crock, 2× fish value + 60), and asks for one item per population milestone (the pond's "ledger"). **Fish traps** (Netter profession, crafted: 40 wood + 3 iron bars): set in any water, checked daily, yield shellfish and bait.

## 11. Economy integration and programmer additions

The dungeon is a second income with higher variance than the farm, capped by Vigor, and the gear it demands is the biggest Marks sink in the game; the 365-day simulation gains a dungeon profile so the two incomes can be tuned against each other.

**Income and sinks.** Expected loot per full-Vigor run (45 floors of Vigor at 6 each): Copper 400–700 ₥, Iron 900–1,500, Gilded 2,000–3,500, Treasury 4,000–6,000, Undercount 6,000–9,000, Deep Stacks scaling 8% per 10 floors; keepers add a one-time 2,000–10,000 ₥ in gems and a unique. Sinks: weapons 1,500 → 40,000 ₥, armor 2,000 → 18,000, Seals 500–3,000, tempering up to 15,000 per weapon, backpacks 12,000 total, rods 4,700, Tonics 60–300 each. Target: a player who farms and dungeon-dives on alternate days earns 1.6× the farm-only income targets in the design spec, and spends 70% of the extra on gear by the Treasury.

**Architecture.** `CombatScene` extends the Vault scene with Arcade physics; every fighter is an entity with `Health`, `Statuses`, `Hitbox`/`Hurtbox`, a `StateMachine` (idle → telegraph → attack → recover → stagger → down) and a behaviour from a small library (chase, dash, ranged, summon, shield, ambush, patrol, swarm) referenced by `enemies.json`; keepers are YAML scripts (`bosses/*.yaml`: phases with HP thresholds, attack patterns, dialogue lines, adds); statuses are one data-driven system (`status.json`: apply, tick, expire, cancel pairs, icons, sounds) shared by players, companions and enemies; loot tables (`loot.yaml`) roll from the day seed plus a run nonce; floors are generated from wing-specific prefab rooms (`dungeon.yaml`: room sets, corridor rules, enemy budget, hazard budget) with a seeded layout so a floor replays identically on the same seed; companions use the NPC pathing with a follow-and-engage behaviour and never a model; projectiles and hit effects are pooled; at most 20 live enemies and 60 projectiles per floor.

**Saves** add `equipment` (weapon, armor, charm, soles, seals, tempering), `spells_known` (with levels), `backpack_tier`, `combat` (Heart, Ink, Arms and Ledgercraft XP, difficulty), `vault` (deepest floor, elevator stops, keepers beaten, pages found, bounties done), `companions` (who, hearts, class, level), `honest_ledger` (counters), `legends` (caught), `fish_pond`, `traps`. Migrations carry the earlier 36-slot inventory into a Deluxe Pack.

**Data files added to `packages/game-data`:** `weapons.json`, `armor.json`, `seals.json`, `spells.json`, `status.json`, `enemies.json`, `bosses/*.yaml`, `dungeon.yaml`, `loot.yaml`, `missions/*.yaml`, `bounties.yaml`, `tonics.json`, `meals.json` (merged into `recipes.json`), `rods.json`, `tackle.json`, `legends.yaml`, `characters/hesper.card.json`, `osric`, `juniper`, `tallow`, and the five keepers' cards.

**Tests.** Deterministic floor generation (same seed, same layout, 500 seeds); the damage formula and every class modifier; status apply/cancel pairs and the no-stacking rule; keeper phase transitions from a fixture; a headless combat simulation that clears each wing with the intended gear (Copper Blade and Arms 1 clears Copper in 20–30 minutes of play; Sterling and Arms 7 clears the Treasury) and fails the build if a wing is unclearable or trivial; the honest-ledger counters never read any event from the bridge; magic refuses to cast in safe zones; Playwright: M1 → M4 end to end on Peaceful, and a legendary catch sequence.

**Art and audio additions.** 22 enemy sprite sets and 5 keepers (16×32 and 32×48, four directions, telegraph frames), 5 weapon classes × 5 tiers as 16×16 icons plus swing frames, spell effects as 8-frame sprites, 12 status icons, 4 new portraits × 6 expressions; 5 wing music loops (one per keeper's office) plus a keeper battle theme in three arrangements and a Deep Stacks ambience; \~80 combat sounds (swing, hit, parry, Stamp, each status apply, each keeper's voice set).

## 12. Phases 10–12 and the added guardrails

**Phase 10 — Combat core and the first two wings.** The combat scene and entity systems, the five weapon classes at Copper and Iron, Arms skill, status effects, the knockout rule, Peaceful and Standard, floor generation, Copper Stacks and Iron Archives with their rosters, the Teller and the Underwriter, Hesper and the Armory, backpacks and the tool belt, M1–M6. AC: (1) the headless combat sim clears Copper with a Copper Blade at Arms 1 in 20–30 minutes and the Teller falls in 3–5 minutes; (2) a knocked-out character wakes at the Chapel at 06:00 with weapons, spells and pages intact and the tithe logged; (3) the same seed generates the same floor 500 times; (4) `pnpm depcruise` and the manifest-hash test still pass with gear equipped.

**Phase 11 — Magic, gear, companions, fishing.** Ledgercraft with all 16 Entries, Ink, the Gilded Reading Room and the Broker, Seals, armor, charms, tempering, Moonsilver forging, Tonics and meals, Juniper and Ada as companions and residents as party members, rods, tackle, bait, the marsh and Vault pools, the fish pond and traps, the first three legendary hunts, C1–C5, M7. AC: (1) every spell casts in the Vault and refuses in safe zones; (2) a created resident completes C5 as a companion and gains the `lore.memories` entry; (3) the Broker's three deals each apply the stated status and the stated drop; (4) The Auditor is catchable only under its stated conditions.

**Phase 12 — Treasury, Undercount, the Receiver and the Deep Stacks.** Wings four and five, the Comptroller and the Receiver, the honest-ledger counters and the Tallies' shield, the third ending, Mister Ink and Marguerite's Trout, Hard Audit, the Deep Stacks with scaling and the lore pages, M8–M10, the secret interactions, Hesper's bounty board. AC: (1) all three endings play from saved games; (2) the honest-ledger counters change only from the listed in-game choices (a test replays a full real-side day of permission events and asserts zero change); (3) Deep Stacks floor 100 is reachable in the sim with Moonsilver gear; (4) Hard Audit grants no Marks the Standard mode does not.

**Added guardrails** (append to `docs/GUARDRAILS.md`):

- [ ] No weapon, Seal, spell, meal, tonic, companion or mission reward reads or writes a limit, tier, allocation, permission or model.
- [ ] Live agents never fight, never join the party, never enter the Vault; companion AI is scripted, never a model.
- [ ] The honest-ledger counters come only from in-game choices; they never read the event store, the audit log or a permission decision.
- [ ] The bounty board and the permission inbox are separate screens with separate data; nothing on the bounty board refers to the real side.
- [ ] Combat never costs real Marks that were minted by the bridge ahead of other Marks: the tithe draws from play-earned Marks first.
- [ ] A knocked-out character is never killed; no save is deleted or rolled back by combat.
